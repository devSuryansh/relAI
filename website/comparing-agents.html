Comparing agents

Definition

A comparison in relAI puts two or more variants on the same tasks, the same number of trials, and the same fault seed, then asks whether their success rates differ by more than chance. The paired test is an exact McNemar test on the runs where the two agents disagreed.

Why this helps you

A table that says 0.77 and 1.00 feels obvious. With a handful of tasks it may be noise, which is what the demo's p-value of 0.50 was telling you. With the reliability suite and five trials, the same kind of gap is 14 disagreements, all in one direction, and the p-value is 0.0001. The interval and the paired test are how you tell those two situations apart before you rewrite your agent.

Try this

relai run reliability --agent naive --agent retry:1 --agent retry:3 --trials 5 --timeout-rate 0.3

What you should see

retry:3  1.00     [1.00, 1.00]  1.00    1.00
retry:1  0.77     [0.67, 0.88]  0.33    0.33
naive    0.58     [0.38, 0.77]  0.25    0.33

retry:3 vs retry:1 on 60 paired runs: retry:3 alone passed 14, retry:1 alone passed 0; McNemar p = 0.0001 (significant)

The full table, including cost and latency, is on Seeded faults. The lines above are the comparison.

How to read it

Variants are ranked by success, then by lower cost, then by lower declared step latency, then by name. The paired sentence always compares the first two in that ranking. Here that is retry:3 against retry:1. Naive is in the table, and it is not in the sentence. Run a command with only the two agents you care about if you want that pair named.

A pair is one task and one trial. Both agents saw it, and because the seed is shared they saw the same faults, as long as they made the same calls. Of the 60 pairs, 14 were passed by retry:3 only. None were passed by retry:1 only. The exact McNemar p-value uses only those discordant pairs. It is two-sided. The lab calls it significant when p is below 0.05.

The interval is about one agent's success rate, not about the gap. retry:1's success sits in [0.67, 0.88]. retry:3's sits at [1.00, 1.00] because every one of the 60 runs passed, and the cluster bootstrap of a perfect score stays at 1. Intervals that do not overlap are a hint. The paired test is the actual comparison, because it keeps the matching faults together.

From Python, the same test is compare(report_a, report_b) in relai.stats. It returns the two names, the pair counts (both, a_only, b_only, neither), and the p-value. significant() uses 0.05 unless you pass another alpha.

Easy to mix up

A significant result says the success counts differ. It does not say the winner is cheaper, or that its failure labels are healthier. retry:3 wins the paired test and still carries 12 horizon flags. Read the failure line beside the p-value. Also, changing --seed or the timeout rate between two separate commands is not a paired comparison. Both agents have to be in the same command so they share the schedule.
